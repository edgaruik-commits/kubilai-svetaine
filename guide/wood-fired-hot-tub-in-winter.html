Using your wood-fired hot tub in winter

A hot bath in the snow is something special. How to heat your hot tub properly in the cold and protect it from frost.

Hot water, cold air and snow on the trees: many of our customers use their hot tub even more in winter than in summer. To keep bathing in frost relaxing and to make sure the tub comes to no harm, there are a few things to keep in mind. Here they are in one place.

Heating up in the cold

In winter the water starts off colder, and the air and wind draw more heat out of the tub, so heating up takes longer than in summer. Our guide heating times depend on the starting temperature of the water, the weather, the wind and whether the cover stays closed.

How to make it quicker and use less wood:

  • Cover on: always keep the thermo cover closed while heating.
  • Dry firewood: damp wood gives less heat and more soot.
  • The right stove output: for the large tub or frequent use in cold weather we recommend the 30 kW stove.
  • A sheltered spot: a place out of the wind, ideally under a roof, helps with heating too.
  • Electric heater as a top-up: a 3 kW electric heater can keep the water warm between baths. It heats slowly, so when you want hot water quickly, light the stove.

Important: always fill the tub with water before you light the fire, and never heat it when the water level is below the minimum. In cold weather, switch on hydro and air massage only once the water has reached +30 °C.

The thermo cover: essential in winter

The insulated thermo cover keeps the heat in the water, shortens the heating time and saves firewood. In winter it matters even more:

  • Keep the cover closed whenever the tub is not in use, and always while the stove is heating.
  • Clear snow off the cover so that it is not overloaded and does not break.
  • Do not sit or stand on the cover, and keep children and pets off it.
  • Wipe the underside dry from time to time.

Bathing safely in frost

The recommended bathing temperature is 36–40 °C; do not go above 40 °C. The built-in thermometer shows you the temperature before you get in. The water near the stove is warmer, so stir it before you rely on the reading.

Take extra care with the steps in winter: wet wood, snow and ice can be slippery, so keep the steps clear and safe. Never leave a burning stove unattended, keep children away from hot surfaces and do not use the chimney pipe as a handle; it stays hot for a long time, even after the fire has gone out.

Extras that pay off in winter

Some extras make bathing in the cold season much more pleasant:

  • Thermo cover: the most important item for winter. It cuts heat loss, shortens the heating time and saves firewood.
  • Stove with a window: you can see the fire and control it more easily. For the large tub and frequent use in the cold we recommend 30 kW.
  • Wi-Fi control with electric heater: together with the 3 kW electric heater you can switch on the heating before you get home. Remote control is for convenience only: never light the stove, and never let the tub heat, without someone nearby.
  • LED lighting: on dark winter evenings it adds atmosphere and makes the steps and surroundings easier to see.
  • Chimney guard: keeps hands and clothing away from the hot pipe, which is especially handy when you walk around the tub in thick winter clothes.
  • Air massage: air cools the water faster. Put the thermo cover on when the air massage is not running.

Frost: the biggest danger for your hot tub

If water freezes in the tub or in the pipes, it can damage the liner and the equipment. Frost damage is not covered as a defect, so prevention is doubly worth it.

  • Drain the stove: when temperatures are below zero, drain the water from the stove after bathing, or the stove can crack.
  • No water in frost without heating: if the outside temperature is below zero and the water in the tub is not kept above 0 °C, no water may stay in the tub. Drain the tub and the filtration system completely.
  • Always wait until the stove has cooled down completely before draining the water.
  • Drain the hydro massage and air system as described in the user manual: after draining, switch the hydro massage on several times for about 3 seconds and the air massage for up to 30 seconds so that the remaining water comes out. Without water, the hydro massage must not run for longer than 3 seconds.

Winter break: putting the hot tub to bed

If you will not use the tub for a few weeks, prepare it like this:

  1. Let the stove cool down and remove the ash.
  2. Drain the tub and the filtration system completely.
  3. Drain the hydro massage and air system as described in the user manual.
  4. Rinse the liner with clean water, wipe it with a soft damp cloth and let it dry.
  5. Let the stove dry so that it does not rust.
  6. Close the thermo cover and clear the snow off it regularly.
  7. Take the headrests off and store them indoors.

Wood and weather in winter

Wood is a natural material. Snow, rain and moisture can make the wooden cladding swell, shrink or warp. That is why we recommend a well-ventilated spot, ideally under a roof, and a foundation that lifts the tub off the ground so that the base has no direct contact with soil, moisture or snow. Treat the wooden cladding with oil or a wood protector before winter; tips are in Caring for and cleaning your hot tub.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use my hot tub in winter?

Yes. What matters is a closed thermo cover while heating, dry firewood and protection from frost when the tub is not heated.

Does heating take longer in winter?

Yes. The heating time depends on the starting temperature of the water, the weather, the wind and the cover. In the cold it takes longer than in summer.

Do I have to drain the water in winter?

When temperatures are below zero, always drain the water from the stove after bathing, or it can crack. If the water in the tub is not kept above 0 °C, drain the tub and the filtration system as well. Wait until the stove has cooled down completely before draining.

When can I switch on the massage in winter?

In cold weather, only once the water has reached +30 °C.

Which stove output is better for winter?

For the large tub or frequent use in the cold we recommend the 30 kW stove.

What should I do with snow on the cover?

Clear it off, so the thermo cover is not overloaded and cannot break.
