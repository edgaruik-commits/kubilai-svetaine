Buying a wood-fired hot tub: what to check first

Size, shape, materials, stove, extras and where it will stand: the points worth settling before you order a hot tub with a wood stove.

A wood-fired hot tub is something you buy for many years of use. To make sure it suits your garden, your household and the way you like to relax, it pays to think a few questions through before you order. This guide goes through them one by one, in the same order we discuss them with our customers.

1. The right size: how many people will use it?

Choose the size by the number of people who will bathe most often, not by the number of guests who visit once a year. A bigger tub holds more water, so it needs more firewood and more time to heat up.

We build two sizes:

  • King M: 185×85 cm inside, 205×100 cm outside, for 5–6 people, approx. 1,100 litres of water
  • King L: 200×90 cm inside, 225×105 cm outside, for 6–8 people, approx. 1,500 litres of water

For a couple or a small family, King M is usually enough: it heats up faster and uses less wood. If friends often join you or the family is larger, King L is the better choice. Either way, leave space around the tub for the stove, the steps and access for maintenance.

2. Round or square?

The shape decides how the tub looks in your garden and how you sit in it. A round hot tub is the classic: everyone faces each other and the heat spreads evenly. A square hot tub has a compact footprint and fits well into a corner, against a wall or on a deck, as long as the clearances for the stove and chimney are kept.

You also choose the inner shape, meaning the shape of the seats moulded into the liner. Not every inner shape works with every stove position; the configurator shows for each shape whether it is made for an internal or an external stove.

3. The liner: acrylic or gelcoat

The liner is the surface you sit on and the shell that holds the water. Gelcoat is hard-wearing, can be repaired and is included in the price. Acrylic is especially smooth, pleasant on the skin and the easiest to clean; it is available at extra cost. Important: a hot tub with an acrylic liner must stand under a roof (a covered terrace, a shelter or a gazebo), protected from direct sun, rain and snow. A hot tub with a gelcoat liner can stand in the open. For a full comparison, see our guide Thermowood, acrylic or gelcoat.

4. The cladding: thermowood or WPC

The outer cladding gives the hot tub its look. Thermowood looks natural and is included in the price; it should be oiled about once a year. WPC (wood-plastic composite) is available at extra cost, needs no painting and very little care: washing it with water and a soft brush is enough. Both come in several shades, from light wood to black.

5. Heating: wood stove inside or outside

A wood-burning stove heats the water without any mains connection. With us you put the stove together step by step:

  • Output: 20 kW or 30 kW. The stronger stove heats a large tub faster and is worth it if you bathe often in the colder months.
  • Stainless steel: AISI 304, or the more corrosion-resistant AISI 316.
  • Window: with or without. With a window you can see the fire and control it more easily.
  • Position: freestanding outside the tub, or inside it behind a wooden guard.
  • Optionally, a 90° pipe bend at the chimney connection.

If an open fire is not allowed where you live, or you simply don't want one, you can choose a 9 kW electric heater instead of the wood stove (a power supply is needed). An additional 3 kW electric heater can keep the water warm between baths. A spark arrestor is always fitted to the chimney; a chimney guard and a 1 m chimney extension are optional.

6. Extras: what is really worth having?

Many extras make bathing more comfortable. To choose, ask yourself how often and for how long you will use the tub:

  • Thermo cover: we recommend it for every hot tub. It cuts heat loss, shortens the heating time, saves firewood and keeps the water clean.
  • Filtration: a filter box or a sand filter keeps the water clear between water changes, which makes sense if you use the tub regularly.
  • Hydro and air massage: water jets massage your back and shoulders, air nozzles give a gentle bubble bath. Both need a power supply.
  • LED lighting, a drink shelf, headrests and Wi-Fi control are comfort extras you can pick to taste.
  • Steps and water thermometer: closed wooden steps are included, and so is the built-in electronic thermometer.

7. Location, foundation and permission

A filled hot tub weighs well over a tonne. It needs a level, load-bearing base where water cannot collect. How to plan the right base is explained in our guide What foundation does a hot tub need?

Before you order, also find out whether the tub and its chimney need a permit or have to be reported in your country, region or municipality, and follow the local rules on fire safety, clearances and emissions. Check local rules with your chimney sweep or local authority. On request we can send you a checklist for your country, but it does not replace the answer from your local authority.

8. Delivery, payment and your rights

Every hot tub is built to your configuration. Production usually takes 1–3 weeks after we receive the deposit, followed by transport, which within Europe usually takes 5–10 working days. The tub is delivered on a pallet to the kerb; unloading, moving it to its place and setting it up are up to you unless agreed otherwise. So plan for helpers or lifting equipment and make sure a lorry can reach the delivery address.

Payment is by bank transfer: a 50% deposit after our order confirmation and the rest before dispatch. Prices in the configurator are not binding; we confirm the final price, delivery cost and delivery date in writing.

As a consumer you have a 14-day right of withdrawal; hot tubs made only from the standard options of our configurator can be returned. The 2-year statutory warranty also applies. For deliveries outside the EU, for example to Switzerland or the United Kingdom, import duties and customs clearance are paid by the buyer; we help with the documents.

Checklist before you order

  • How many people will usually bathe? → King M or King L
  • Where will the tub stand, and is the base level and load-bearing?
  • Round or square, and does the inner shape suit the stove position you want?
  • Acrylic or gelcoat, thermowood or WPC? (Acrylic only with a roof over the tub)
  • Wood stove (20 or 30 kW, inside or outside) or electric heater?
  • Thermo cover and filtration included in your plan?
  • Permission, clearances and chimney checked with your chimney sweep or local authority?
  • Can a lorry reach the delivery address, and who will help unload?

Frequently asked questions

How long does the water take to heat up?

As a guide, allow about 2.5 hours for King M and about 3 hours for King L. The actual time depends on the starting temperature of the water, the weather, the wind and whether the thermo cover stays closed.

Does a hot tub with a wood stove need electricity?

Not for the wood stove. Power is only needed for electric extras such as hydro and air massage, LED lighting, Wi-Fi control or the electric heater. These must be connected by a qualified electrician in line with local regulations.

How long does delivery take?

Production usually takes 1–3 weeks after we receive the deposit, and transport within Europe usually 5–10 working days. We give you the binding delivery date in the order confirmation.

What warranty do I get?

The 2-year statutory warranty applies from delivery. We do not give an additional manufacturer's guarantee.

Can I return my hot tub?

As a consumer you have a 14-day right of withdrawal. The only exception is a tub made to your individual specifications that we have expressly described as such in the offer. The details are in our withdrawal information.
