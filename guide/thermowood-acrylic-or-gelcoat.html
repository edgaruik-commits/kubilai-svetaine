Thermowood, acrylic or gelcoat: which material suits you?

Acrylic or gelcoat liner, thermowood or WPC cladding: how they differ, how to care for them and which combination suits you.

With a hot tub with a wood stove you choose two materials: the liner, which holds the water and which you sit on, and the outer cladding, which gives the tub its look. Both affect how bathing feels, how much care is needed and how the tub fits into your garden. In this guide we compare the options we offer from our workshop.

The liner: acrylic or gelcoat

The liner is the heart of the hot tub. It holds the water, forms the seats and is the surface you touch when you bathe. We make it from two materials.

Gelcoat

Gelcoat is a hard-wearing surface layer that can be repaired if needed. It is included in the price, which makes it the sensible choice if you want to keep the cost down. For the round Shape 1, gelcoat also comes in special finishes: Grey pearl and Blue pearl with a pearl sheen, and Granite 3D in Obsidian, Moonstone and Gold dust.

Acrylic

Acrylic is especially smooth and pleasant on the skin. It is the easiest to clean and therefore the first choice if you want the smoothest and most comfortable surface. Acrylic is available at extra cost.

Important: a hot tub with an acrylic liner must stand under a roof (a covered terrace, a shelter or a gazebo), protected from direct sun, rain and snow. A hot tub with a gelcoat liner can stand in the open.

Colours

Which colours are available depends on the inner shape:

  • Round Shape 1: gelcoat and acrylic in RAL 7015 (slate grey), RAL 5012 (light blue) and RAL 9001 (cream white); gelcoat also in pearl and Granite 3D finishes.
  • All other inner shapes: gelcoat and acrylic in Ice blue, Pearl white, Graphite, Mocha brown, Sky blue, Deep blue, Sand beige and Sage green.

The configurator shows exactly the colours available for each shape. A practical tip: limescale marks show up more on dark colours, but they are easy to remove. Graphite is forgiving with dirt and goes especially well with dark wood.

Caring for the liner

The same applies to both materials: only clean water and a soft damp cloth. No cleaning products, no chlorine, no solvents such as acetone or thinners, and nothing abrasive. More in our guide Caring for and cleaning your hot tub.

The cladding: thermowood or WPC

The cladding is what you and your guests see of the hot tub. It is held together by stainless steel bands and is exposed to the weather.

Thermowood

Thermowood is heat-treated wood. It looks natural and warm, is included in the price and is well suited to outdoor use; we expressly recommend heat-treated wood to keep the effects of the weather to a minimum. Like any wood, it changes colour over time, especially in direct sun. Oil it or treat it with a wood protector once or twice a year to keep the colour and protect the wood from moisture.

WPC

WPC is a composite of wood and plastic. It needs no painting or oiling and hardly any care: washing it with water and a soft brush is enough. WPC is available at extra cost and is the right choice if you don't want to look after anything.

Shades

We offer eight shades for the cladding, the same for thermowood and WPC: Spruce, Thermowood, Larch, Natural pine, Red cedar, Walnut, Grey wash and Black stain. The steps match the cladding in wood tone.

Matching the colours

A hot tub looks calmest when the liner, cladding and accessories go together. A few tips from experience:

  • Liner: light colours look fresh and make the water appear clear; dark colours such as Graphite are forgiving with dirt but show limescale marks more clearly.
  • Thermo cover: available in black, brown and grey. Choose a colour that goes with the cladding.
  • Steps and drink shelf: the wooden steps match the cladding in wood tone. The drink shelf comes in wood to match the cladding, or in dark metal, which is especially easy to wipe clean.
  • Stove: the stove is made of stainless steel, either AISI 304 or the more corrosion-resistant AISI 316. With a window, the fire itself becomes part of the design.

In the 3D configurator you can see every combination from every angle, by day and by night, with or without water. Bear in mind that it is a visualisation: the actual hot tub may differ in colour, shape and details.

Which combination suits you?

  • Natural and budget-conscious: gelcoat liner with thermowood cladding. Both are included in the price, and the tub can stand in the open.
  • Maximum comfort: an acrylic liner for the smoothest surface and the easiest cleaning, with a roof over the tub.
  • As little care as possible: acrylic inside, WPC outside. Ideal if you want to enjoy the tub rather than look after it, and you have a covered spot.
  • Dark, modern look: a Graphite liner with dark wood such as Walnut or Black stain.

Whatever the material, nothing protects the cladding as well as the right spot: well ventilated, ideally under a roof and on a foundation that lifts the tub off the ground. Frequently asked questions

Which is better, acrylic or gelcoat?

Acrylic is smoother, more pleasant on the skin and the easiest to clean; it costs extra, and the tub must stand under a roof. Gelcoat is hard-wearing, repairable, included in the price and can stand in the open.

Does thermowood need looking after?

Yes, oil it or treat it with a wood protector once or twice a year. That keeps the colour and protects the wood from moisture.

What is WPC?

A composite of wood and plastic used for the outer cladding. It needs hardly any care and is available at extra cost.

Does the bath water touch the wood?

No. The water is held in the acrylic or gelcoat liner; the wood or WPC only forms the outer cladding.
