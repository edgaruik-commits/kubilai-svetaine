Learn everything about how a hot tub is built and how to use it: from size and shape to the stove, massage, lighting, heating and control. All 22 parts of the tub on one picture, each with what you can choose, how to use and care for it, and our recommendation.

What's included

Every part of a Forest Kings hot tub at a glance. Click a number on the picture or in the list to see what the part is, what you can choose and how to use and care for it.

1Chimney and spark arrestor

The chimney carries smoke away from the stove and creates the draught for a clean fire. A spark arrestor is always fitted at the top.

What you can choose

  • Spark arrestor: always fitted; stops sparks leaving the chimney and settling on a terrace or garden
  • Extra chimney, +1 m: an additional metre of chimney pipe
  • A taller chimney creates a stronger draught, so the fire lights easily, burns cleaner and smoke does not blow back into the stove
  • Smoke and sparks then leave higher, away from the people in the tub, the terrace and nearby windows or roofs
  • The extra metre is useful when the tub stands close to a wall, hedge, tree or roof, or in a windy spot, because the top of the chimney then sits above the obstacle

Use and care

  • Keep the safe distance from walls, roofs, trees and other flammable materials, as set out in the manual
  • Clean the chimney and the spark arrestor regularly; soot reduces the draught
  • Do not use the chimney tube as a handle: it stays hot long after the fire is out

Our recommendation

Choose the extra 1 m if there is a wall, roof or tree near the tub, or if the spot is windy.

2Chimney guard

A protective cover around the chimney that keeps hands and clothing away from the hot pipe.

What you can choose

  • Chimney guard for the chimney of the wood-fired stove
  • Not needed with the electric 9 kW heater: it has no fire and no chimney

Use and care

  • Do not leave a burning stove unattended, and keep children away from the hot surfaces
  • Do not use the chimney tube as a handle: it stays hot long after the fire is out

Our recommendation

We recommend adding the chimney guard for a family garden or a wooden terrace.

3Wood-fired stove

A wood-fired stove heats the water, so the tub works without a power connection. Choose the model step by step: power, metal and glass, with an optional elbow. An electric 9 kW heater is available instead of the wood-fired stove.

What you can choose

  • Power: 20 kW or 30 kW (heats a large tub faster)
  • Metal: stainless steel AISI 304 or AISI 316 (higher corrosion resistance)
  • With glass (you see the fire) or without glass
  • Optional 90° elbow on the chimney connection
  • Position: outside (free-standing) or inside, behind a wooden guard
  • Electric 9 kW heater: no fire and no chimney, needs a power connection

Use and care

  • Fill the tub with water first, leaving 10–15 cm free at the top, and only then light the stove; never let the stove run with water below the minimum level
  • Use dry firewood; wet wood gives less heat and more soot
  • Do not put in too much fuel, so that the fire does not come out through the door
  • Do not leave a burning stove unattended, and keep children away from the hot surfaces
  • Remove ash only when the stove is cold; let the stove dry after use to avoid rust
  • Drain the water only when the stove has cooled down completely
  • At sub-zero temperatures always drain the water from the stove after use, otherwise the stove will crack
  • The electric heater must be connected to local regulations by a qualified electrician

Our recommendation

A 20 kW stove with glass in AISI 316. Glass lets you watch and control the fire and AISI 316 resists corrosion best. Choose 30 kW for the large tub or for frequent use in cold weather. Choose the electric 9 kW heater if an open fire is not allowed or not wanted at your place.

4Stove connection

The external stove is joined to the tub with stainless steel connections and hoses.

What you can choose

  • Stainless steel stove connections, made in our own workshop
  • Stove position: outside (free-standing) or inside, behind a wooden guard

Use and care

  • Place the stove on a level, firm base, with its front slightly lower
  • The connection hoses between the tub and the stove must not run upwards; they must run downwards
  • Use only clean water: iron and limescale deposits prevent gates and valves from closing tightly and clog the equipment
  • If the iron content of the water exceeds 200 µg/l, install an iron-removal filter; if the water is hard, a softening / descaling filter is required
5Inner shell

The inner shell is the surface you sit on and the part that holds the water. It is available in gelcoat or in acrylic, in three colours.

What you can choose

  • Gelcoat: robust and repairable
  • Acrylic: very smooth and pleasant on the skin
  • Colours: Graphite, Mocha brown, Deep blue (the same three for gelcoat and acrylic)
  • A tub with an acrylic liner must stand under a roof (terrace with a roof, shelter or gazebo); a gelcoat liner may also stand outdoors

Use and care

  • Clean only with clean water and a soft, damp cloth; do not use any cleaning products, solvents or scouring pads
  • Rinse away sunscreen, oils and soap residue regularly
  • Dark colours show limescale lines more; they clean off easily
  • Do not use glass containers or hard and sharp objects in the tub, and do not climb in with footwear

Our recommendation

Choose acrylic if you want the smoothest, most comfortable surface and easiest cleaning. Gelcoat is the sensible choice if you want to keep the price down. Graphite hides dirt and looks best next to dark wood.

6Size, shape and seats

Two sizes and two shapes, built to order in our own workshop. Pick the size by the number of people who will use the tub most often, and by the space you have for it and the steps. The seats are shaped in the inner shell.

What you can choose

  • King M: inner 185×85 cm, outer 205×100 cm, 5–6 people, about 1,100 l of water, heat-up in about 2.5 h
  • King L: inner 200×90 cm, outer 225×105 cm, 6–8 people, about 1,500 l of water, heat-up in about 3 h
  • Round: the classic shape, every seat has a view of the others
  • Square: compact outer footprint, fits corners and terraces
  • Inner shape for round tubs: Shape 1 (classic seat bench around a central well) or Shape 2 (moulded 3D liner with shaped seats)
  • Air massage nozzles can be fitted in the seats (point 9) and headrests on the rim above them (point 7)

Use and care

  • Allow free space around the tub for the stove, the steps and for service access
  • Prepare a level, load-bearing base; a full tub weighs well over a tonne
  • Round tubs sit best on a free-standing base; square ones fit well against a wall or fence (keep the clearances for the stove and chimney)
  • The shape does not change the heating principle, only the water volume and the layout of seats and accessories
  • Larger tubs hold more water and need more firewood and more time to heat
  • Heat-up times are guide values: they depend on the starting water temperature, the weather, the wind and whether the cover stays on
  • Do not sit on the edge of the tub; recommended bathing temperature is 36–40 °C, do not exceed 40 °C

Our recommendation

For a family or regular visits with friends choose King L (6–8 people). For a couple or a small family, King M is enough and heats faster with less firewood. Round is our recommendation for the most comfortable seating and the best heat distribution. Choose square if the tub has to fit a corner or sit against a wall.

7Headrests

Soft pillows that hang over the rim and give your head and neck a comfortable rest while you relax.

What you can choose

  • 1 to 8 headrests
  • Dark colour, hangs over the rim of the tub

Use and care

  • Rinse with clean water after use and let them dry
  • Take the headrests off and store them indoors in winter
  • Do not use them as a step or handle

Our recommendation

One headrest per regular seat is ideal; for a family tub 4 headrests cover the most used places.

8Hydro massage system

Water jets in the shell massage your back, hips and feet. The jets are driven by a pump (electric connection needed).

What you can choose

  • 8 or 12 jets
  • More jets cover more of the body and give a stronger massage
  • Jet placement: standard (our proven layout) or individual (you tell us where you want them)

Use and care

  • Always keep the water level above the jets; never run the pump without water for longer than 3 seconds
  • Switch it on and off with the buttons and set the strength with the flow regulator; keep the regulator at MIN when the massage is not in use
  • Turn the jet nozzles to change the direction and strength of the flow
  • Clean or replace the filter regularly so that the pump stays quiet and efficient
  • In cold weather switch the massage on only when the water has warmed up to +30 °C
  • After draining, run the hydro massage for about 3 seconds, several times, so that the remaining water leaves the system
  • Electrical parts are CE-marked; the installation must be done to local regulations by a qualified electrician

Our recommendation

For two or three people 8 jets are comfortable; for a full family tub choose 12 jets. Standard placement works for most people; choose individual if you have a favourite seat or need the jets at a particular height. Hydro massage is the best match for back and shoulders.

9Air massage system

Air bubbles are blown into the water through nozzles in the seats. It gives a soft, bubbling massage that feels different from the jets.

What you can choose

  • 12 nozzles with 750 W, in the seats
  • 24 nozzles: the 12 in the seats plus 12 in the back rest
  • Nozzle placement: standard or individual

Use and care

  • Start with the lowest setting and increase gradually
  • Air cools the water faster; use the thermal cover when the air system is not running
  • Do not use soaps or bath foams in a tub with an air system
  • In cold weather switch the air massage on only when the water has warmed up to +30 °C
  • After draining, run the air massage for up to 30 seconds so that the remaining water leaves the system

Our recommendation

Choose 12 nozzles for a gentle bubble bath. Take 24 nozzles if the air massage is one of the main reasons you are buying the tub, as the back rest nozzles add a massage for the back.

10LED RGB lighting

Colour lighting in the tub wall turns an evening bath into an experience and makes steps and surroundings easier to see.

What you can choose

  • LED RGB lighting: 2, 3 or 5 lamps
  • Lamp placement: standard (spread evenly round the tub, shown in the configurator as clock hours) or individual

Use and care

  • Switch the lighting on and off with the buttons on the outer wall of the tub
  • Use warm and slow colour changes for relaxing, brighter white for cleaning and checks
  • Turn the light off when the tub is not in use
  • Do not open or repair the lighting yourself; contact us

Our recommendation

3 RGB lamps for a full, even glow, 5 for the large tub.

11LED Mini lighting

Small LED lights for a soft accent light: a separate option that can be added on its own or together with the RGB lamps.

What you can choose

  • 10 or 20 small LED lights
  • On its own or together with the LED RGB lighting

Use and care

  • Switch the lighting on and off with the buttons on the outer wall of the tub
  • Turn the light off when the tub is not in use
  • Do not open or repair the lighting yourself; contact us

Our recommendation

LED Mini is enough if you only want a soft accent light.

12Drink shelf

A shelf on the rim keeps your drink, phone or towel within reach.

What you can choose

  • Wooden shelf (matches the cladding)
  • Metal shelf (dark, easy to wipe clean)

Use and care

  • Use only unbreakable cups at the tub
  • Do not load the shelf with heavy items
  • Wipe the wooden shelf dry and oil it with the cladding

Our recommendation

Choose the wooden shelf for a natural look next to wood cladding, or the metal one if you want the easiest cleaning. One shelf is usually enough for two to four people.

13Control buttons

Push buttons on the outer wall of the tub switch the hydro massage, the air massage and the LED lighting on and off.

What you can choose

  • Stainless steel push-button panel on the tub wall
  • Position: you choose where around the tub the panel is mounted
  • The display of the integrated thermometer sits in the same panel (point 14)

Use and care

  • The strength of the massage flow is set with a regulator; keep it at MIN when the massage is not in use, otherwise it lets water through
  • Connect the tub directly to an automatic circuit breaker and use a residual-current device (RCD) for additional protection
  • Make sure that the installation is earthed (grounded)
14Water thermometer

Know the water temperature before you step in.

What you can choose

  • Integrated electronic thermometer: a small display on the outside wall

Use and care

  • Recommended bathing temperature is 36–40 °C; do not exceed 40 °C
  • Check the reading before you get in, and again while the stove is burning
  • Water near the stove is warmer: stir the water before you rely on the reading

Our recommendation

We recommend the integrated electronic thermometer: always visible, nothing floating in the water.

15Control (Wi-Fi)

Control your hot tub from your phone: check and adjust functions without walking to the tub.

What you can choose

  • Switch functions on and off via Wi-Fi
  • Monitor from anywhere on your home network

Use and care

  • Place the tub within range of the Wi-Fi network or use an extender
  • Use a strong password for the connection
  • Remote control is for convenience only: never light the stove or leave the tub heating without someone nearby

Our recommendation

Add Wi-Fi control if you want to check and adjust the tub from the house or from work. If you only visit the tub occasionally and are always nearby, you can do without it.

16Steps

Wooden steps make getting in and out safe. Closed steps come with every tub, and you choose where around the tub they stand.

What you can choose

  • Closed steps: solid sides
  • Individual order: steps made to your own size or design
  • Position: pick the clock hour around the tub where the steps stand, so that they do not clash with the stove or other parts
  • Wood tone matches the cladding

Use and care

  • Place the steps on a firm, level base so that they do not rock
  • Wet wood can be slippery: brush off algae and leaves
  • Treat the wood with oil once or twice a year

Our recommendation

The closed steps suit most gardens. Choose an individual order if the base is uneven, the tub stands on a terrace edge, or you want steps of a special height or width.

17Cladding

The outer cladding gives the tub its look. Choose real thermo wood or a low-maintenance WPC composite, in eight tones.

What you can choose

  • Thermo wood or WPC composite
  • Spruce, Thermowood, Larch, Natural pine, Red cedar, Walnut, Grey wash and Black stain

Use and care

  • Wood is a natural material: colour will weather over time, especially in direct sun
  • In moisture wood expands and when drying it shrinks: keep the tub in a dry, well-ventilated place; a roof or shelter is always recommended
  • Re-oil or re-treat wooden cladding once or twice a year to keep the colour and protect it from moisture
  • WPC needs little care: wash with water and a soft brush

Our recommendation

For long life with little work choose thermo wood or WPC. Thermo wood gives a natural look and needs an annual oiling; WPC suits you if you do not want to do any maintenance.

18Frame and stainless steel hoops

The frame is the load-bearing construction under the cladding: the structure that keeps the shell in shape and holds the stove, the steps and the accessories in place.

What you can choose

  • Built in our own workshop, made to order
  • Stainless steel stove connections and a wooden outer construction
  • Stainless steel hoops hold the cladding together

Use and care

  • Place the tub on a level, horizontal and firm base (concrete slab, compacted gravel with paving, or a terrace built for the load)
  • Keep the base drained, so that water does not collect under the tub
  • Check the hoops and screws once a season

Our recommendation

Give the tub a level, solid base of concrete or paving and keep the area under it drained. A good base protects the frame more than anything else.

19Thermal cover

An insulated lid that keeps the heat in, keeps leaves and dirt out and protects the water between baths.

What you can choose

  • Thermo cover in three colours: Black, Brown, Grey
  • Folds in the middle for easy opening
  • Insulated, so heat loss between baths is lower

Use and care

  • Keep the cover closed when the tub is not in use, and always when the stove is heating
  • Do not sit or stand on the cover and keep children and pets off it
  • Brush off snow, rain and leaves; wipe the underside dry from time to time

Our recommendation

We strongly recommend the thermal cover for every tub: it cuts heat loss, shortens heat-up time, saves firewood and keeps the water clean. Choose a colour that matches the cladding.

20Stove door with glass

A stove with glass lets you watch the fire and control it.

What you can choose

  • Stove with glass (you see the fire) or without glass
  • Combined with the power and the metal you choose for the stove (point 3)

Use and care

  • Adjust the draught with the vent on the stove door or with the ash drawer
  • Do not put too much fuel into the stove, so that the fire does not come out through the door
  • During the first and second heating, condensation may appear in the firebox; after the third firing it no longer appears
  • Remove ash only when the stove is cold; let the stove dry after use to avoid rust
21Filtration system

A filtration system keeps the water clear between water changes. You can choose a compact filter box or a sand filter.

What you can choose

  • Filter box: a wooden cabinet next to the tub, in a square or a round version that follows the shape of the tub
  • Sand filter: a stronger system for larger water volumes
  • Filter connection: only the connection fitted in the tub, if you want to add your own filter later

Use and care

  • Clean or backwash the filter on a regular schedule, as set out in the manual
  • Use only clean water without chemicals or salts: no chlorine, no bath foam and no other additives
  • Drain the tub and the filtration system in winter if the tub is not heated, to avoid frost damage
  • Even with a filter, change the water every few weeks, depending on use

Our recommendation

Choose the filter box for regular use: it keeps the water clear and saves frequent water changes. Choose the round box if the tub is round so the box sits flush against it. Choose a sand filter for heavy use and larger groups.

22Electric heating 3 kW

A compact 3 kW electric heater that warms the water with electricity, without lighting a fire. It needs a power connection.

What you can choose

  • Electric heating system, 3 kW
  • Can be added to a tub with a wood-fired stove

Use and care

  • The heater must be connected to local regulations by a qualified electrician
  • Never switch the heater on with the water below the minimum level
  • Keep the thermal cover on while heating to save energy
  • 3 kW heats slowly; light the stove when you need the water hot quickly

Our recommendation

Add the 3 kW electric heater if you want to keep the water warm between baths or top up the temperature without lighting the stove. Combined with Wi-Fi control, you can switch it on before you get home.
